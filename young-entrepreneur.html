The problem

Everyone Tells Kids to “Be an Entrepreneur.” Nobody Shows Them the Steps.

Your child hears it constantly — start something, build something, be your own boss. What they never get is the part in between: how you actually go from a vague idea to a real person paying you for it.

  • They're told to “find a problem to solve” — but not how to spot one.
  • They're told to “know your customer” — but not how to work out who that is.
  • They're told to “price it right” — but nobody shows them the maths.

This playbook is the step-by-step. Eight chapters, each one followed by an activity your child fills in — so the idea turns into a plan they can actually run.

What's Inside

Eight chapters, in order. Each one ends with an activity your child completes before moving on.

01

What Is an Entrepreneur?

Strip the word back to what it actually means: spotting a problem and solving it for a price.

02

The Entrepreneur Mindset

How to treat a “no” and a mistake as information rather than a reason to stop.

03

Finding Your Opportunity

Turn everyday frustrations — your own and other people's — into a list of real openings.

04

Who Are You Building For?

Build one specific customer in detail, so your child stops guessing what people want.

05

Building Your Business Brain

How the pieces fit together: what you offer, what it costs you, and what makes it work.

06

Money and Your Business

Startup costs, what you earn per sale, and the point where the business pays for itself.

07

Your First Customer

How to ask — the words, the person, and what to do with the answer either way.

08

Growing What Works

Read what the first month actually told you, then decide what to repeat and what to drop.

Real Stories page

A look at businesses other young people have started, and what each one did first.

“My Blueprint” plan page

One page that pulls every activity together into the plan your child will actually run.

Knowledge check

A short check at the end so your child can see what landed and what to revisit.

Certificate

Something to sign and keep when they've finished all eight chapters.
